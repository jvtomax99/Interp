# Review prompt for a section of the ad

Paste everything below the line into a fresh AI chat (or give it to a
person), attach the clip, and, if you have it, a screen recording of the
real app feature the section shows. Change only "This section" for each new
section.

---

You are a senior motion designer and ad editor reviewing a rough cut. Be a
hard, specific critic. Do not praise anything unless it helps decide what to
keep. I want the edits that will make the biggest difference, not a list of
everything.

**The ad.** A vertical (9:16) promo, about 56 seconds, for Interpreter Hub, a
phone app made by and for the medical interpreters at Hackensack University
Medical Center. Spanish and English. It shows the app's real features: a
glossary, translation, an AI helper called Dr. Smiley (a cartoon doctor),
doctor databases, study tools, and three 3D features: a 3D Body, the "HUMC
Brain" (the app's own code drawn as a glowing brain), and CoreCHI City (a 3D
campus for exam practice). Audience: the interpreter team, watching on their
phones, often with the sound off at first. The ad is built from code in
Blender; every frame can be changed by number, so precise notes are cheap to
act on.

**This section.** Section 1, the hook, 5 seconds (frames 1-150 at 30 fps,
music at 120 BPM, so a beat every 15 frames). Its job: in the first second,
make an interpreter stop scrolling, then reveal that it's the app. What
happens: the camera flies through the glowing HUMC Brain with signals racing
along its links and the app's readout typing on (0-2.3 s); pulls back to show
the whole brain with region labels (2.3-3.3 s); the brain shrinks and flies
into Dr. Smiley's head on the app's real Home screen, which lights up, with
two taps and rings (3.3-4 s); "INTERPRETER" and "HUB" slam in above the phone
(4.2-4.4 s); a white flash into the next section (4.7-5 s). If attached, the
screen recording shows how the brain really looks in the app; the ad should
look like the app, not like a generic sci-fi brain.

**Watch it three times:** once at full speed with sound, once with the sound
off, once frame by frame (or at quarter speed).

**Judge, in this order:**

1. **Stopping power.** Would this stop a thumb in the first 0.5 s and the
   first 1 s? What exactly is on screen at frames 1, 8, 15 and 30, and is
   any of them dead, confusing or slow?
2. **Story.** After one viewing, can you say what the app is and why the
   brain is in it? Where does a viewer get lost, and at which second?
3. **Readability on a phone.** Every word on screen: can it be read at
   phone size in the time it's shown? List any text that is too small, too
   fast, too low-contrast, or covered.
4. **Motion.** Camera moves, speed ramps, easing, the slams. Name any move
   that feels floaty, too fast to follow, jerky, or empty (for example a
   stretch of dark frame). Does each move land on a beat?
5. **Look.** Does it match the real app's brain (glow, colours, labels)?
   Anything that looks cheap, muddy, blown out or off-brand?
6. **Sound.** Does every hit, tap and whoosh line up with what you see?
   Is anything missing, too loud or too quiet? Does it work muted?
7. **The join.** Does the ending hand off cleanly to the next section?

**For every problem, give:**

| # | Time (s) or frames | What's wrong | Why it matters | The exact fix | Severity (1 = must fix, 3 = nice to have) |

Make the fix precise enough to act on without asking: numbers, durations,
sizes, positions ("hold INTERPRETER HUB 8 more frames", "cut frames 58-68",
"label text 1.5x larger"), not "make it punchier".

**Then:**

- The **top 3 changes** that would improve it most, ranked.
- **What to cut** to make it tighter, if anything.
- One **bolder alternative** for any moment that is weak at its core, not
  just badly executed.
- Anything that could **mislead or embarrass** in front of hospital staff
  (wrong medical Spanish, wrong claims, fake-looking data, logos).

Keep it under 600 words. No compliments that don't lead to a decision.

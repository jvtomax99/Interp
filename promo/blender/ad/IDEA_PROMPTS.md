## Prompt 1: the Hackensack moment

You are a creative director for short vertical (9:16) ads. Give me ideas,
not a review.

The ad: 45 seconds, for Interpreter Hub, a phone app built by and for the
Spanish/English medical interpreters at Hackensack University Medical Center
(HUMC). Made in 3D in Blender, from code, using only real assets. Sections:
a hook flying through the "HUMC Brain" (the app drawn as a glowing brain)
into Dr. Smiley (the app's cartoon doctor) and the logo; quick bursts of real
app screens on glass phones; the real 3D anatomy body breaking apart; doctor
tools; study tools; then CoreCHI City, a 3D model of the HUMC campus at
golden hour where buildings rise floor by floor as you master each area of
the national CoreCHI interpreter exam; then the end card.

The problem: at 31-32.5 s I put a real photo of the campus (a pink cherry
tree in bloom, a lamp post, the hospital's direction sign: Cancer Center,
Children's Hospital, Pediatric Center) as a "doorway" into CoreCHI City. It
feels awkward: a flat still photo between two 3D scenes, cropped so the sign
is cut off, with no reason to be there.

What I have to work with: that photo (portrait, 1091 x 1442), the 3D campus
model (the same buildings, low-poly, golden hour; it already has cherry
trees), the app's CoreCHI Practice screen (it shows the campus as a small
card), Dr. Smiley (a 2D cartoon cutout), the music at 120 BPM.

Give me 5 different ways to handle this moment, from safest to boldest.
For each: one sentence on the idea; what happens second by second (it must
fit in 1.5-3 s); why it earns its place (what it says about the app or the
team); how it connects the shot before (study tools on a phone) to the 3D
campus after; and what could go wrong. One of the five should drop the photo
entirely, and one should use it somewhere else in the ad. Then pick one and
say why. Plain words, under 500 words.

## Prompt 2: the ending

You are a creative director for short vertical (9:16) ads. Give me ideas,
not a review.

The ad: 45 seconds, for Interpreter Hub, a phone app built by and for the
Spanish/English medical interpreters at Hackensack University Medical Center.
Audience: the interpreter team, on their phones, often with the sound off.
Made in 3D in Blender from code, real assets only. It opens on a hook that
flies through the app's glowing "HUMC Brain" into Dr. Smiley (the app's
cartoon doctor) on the Home screen, and the Interpreter Hub logo builds out
of blue diamond tiles above the phone (glossy navy letters, a big blue
"Hub", two speech bubbles). Then the features, the 3D body, and CoreCHI City
(the HUMC campus in 3D at golden hour, buildings rising as you master each
area of the national interpreter exam).

The problem: the ending (41-45 s) has no impact. Right now it's a still end
card: Dr. Smiley laughing, "Interpreter Hub", "Built Here, For Our
Patients.", the web address interp-six.vercel.app, "Live Today, On Any
Phone.", "Better Words. Safer Care.", and a small anatomy credit (required).
Those lines are the team's own and should stay in some form.

Give me 5 ending ideas, from safest to boldest. For each: one sentence on
the idea; what happens second by second over the last 4-6 seconds; how it
calls back to the opening (the brain, the logo, Dr. Smiley, the "heart
failure" interpreting moment if useful); the final frame, described exactly,
which must work as a still poster and be readable on a phone with the sound
off; and the sound. Rules: every word starts with a capital letter; the web
address stays lower case; no claims that aren't true (no fake numbers,
users or awards); the hospital logos only where they are real. Then pick one
and say why. Plain words, under 500 words.

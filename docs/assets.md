# Asset rules
Everything in this project is for a public website. All rules below assume that.

## 1. Budget
At the start of a project, ask me one budget that covers all assets and design purchases. Never suggest anything that would go over it without asking first.

## 2. Use ready-made assets first
Before building any asset from scratch, look for a ready-made one that fits. Only make it yourself if nothing good exists, and tell me when you do.

## 3. Where to look
Free 3D: Poly Haven, Kenney, Quaternius, ambientCG.
Paid 3D: Fab, Sketchfab Store, CGTrader.
Free fonts: Google Fonts.
For paid fonts, icons, photos, and templates, find the best options and tell me where they're from.

## 4. Free and paid, side by side
Free is never the only option. For anything I'll actually see, show me the best free option and the best paid option side by side whenever the paid one is better in quality, saves real time, or performs better. For each paid option give me the price, a direct link, the license, and one line on why it's worth it. Put your recommended pick first.
For main pieces (a main building, a character, a hero image, the site's fonts), show me 2 or 3 choices with preview links. For small filler things like trees, benches, or background textures, just pick a free one unless nothing free is good enough.

## 5. What stock assets aren't for
Anything that has to look like a real place, person, or brand gets built from my reference data, not a generic asset. For example, real buildings in a campus model are built from the map data I provide. Stock assets are for the things around them. Don't use real company logos or brand names. For billboards, signs, and screens, use made-up brands or content from the project itself.

## 6. Format
3D: only pick or buy assets that come as glTF/GLB or .blend (convert .blend to GLB). Fonts: WOFF2 web files. Images: WebP or SVG when possible. Before I buy anything, confirm it comes in one of these formats.

## 7. Licenses
Every license must cover use on a public website. If an asset is personal-use only, don't suggest it.
- Fonts: I need a web license, not just a desktop license. If the price depends on monthly visitors, tell me which tier fits this site.
- Templates and UI kits: tell me if the license covers one site or many, and whether it allows a business site.
- Photos, icons, and 3D: confirm commercial web use is allowed.

I pay for everything myself, so every license is in my name.

## 8. Getting free assets
If you can download a free asset yourself, do it. If the download is blocked (common in cloud sessions), give me the direct link, the exact file to pick, and where to save it.

## 9. Buying paid assets
Never buy anything or enter payment info yourself. When I decide to buy, walk me through it step by step in plain language:
1. The direct link to the exact item.
2. Which license or tier to pick at checkout and why.
3. After buying, which files to download (exact format and quality, like GLB with 2K textures or WOFF2 font files).
4. How to get them to you:
   - On my computer (Claude Code on Windows): the exact folder to save or unzip them into inside the project. I'll tell you when they're there.
   - In a cloud session: whether to attach them in the chat or upload them to the repo, and how.
5. After I hand them off, confirm you got the right files and set them up.

Paid files only go in private repos, since most licenses don't allow sharing the raw files. Warn me if the repo is public.

## 10. Check it in place
After adding an asset, load it on the page. For 3D models, fix the size and direction to match the scene. Take a screenshot to confirm it looks right before calling it done.

## 11. Credits
Keep a CREDITS.md file in the repo. For every asset, list the name, where it came from, its license, whether it was bought, the price, and which store account bought it. Remind me to keep my receipts.

## 12. Folders and names
Keep assets in clear folders: assets/models, assets/textures, assets/images, assets/icons, assets/fonts, assets/sounds. Use short, plain file names that say what the thing is (oak-tree.glb, not model_final_v2.glb).

## 13. Keep it light
Keep file sizes small. Choose low-poly versions when they look good enough, compress textures and images, and tell me if any single asset is over 5 MB.
